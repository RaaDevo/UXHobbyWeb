Colour theory in photography

Use Colour with Intention

Colour can attract attention, create harmony, and influence how a photograph feels before a viewer reads the story.

Learn to notice colour relationships, control white balance, and use the amethyst, blues, and warm tones already present in a scene with purpose.

Explore colour harmonies

Colour basics

Describe what you see

Hue

The basic colour name, such as red, blue, green, or purple. A clear hue can become a visual anchor in a busy photograph.

Saturation

The intensity of a colour. Highly saturated scenes feel vivid; lower saturation can feel quiet, nostalgic, or restrained.

Brightness / value

How light or dark a colour appears. A bright subject against a dark background creates immediate contrast.

Warm colours

Reds, oranges, and yellows can suggest energy, closeness, sunlight, and warmth.

Cool colours

Blues, greens, and blue-violets can suggest calm, distance, reflection, or a colder atmosphere.

Colour harmonies

Build balanced combinations

Select a harmony to see how its palette can shape a photograph.

Selected

Complementary

Colours opposite on a colour wheel, such as blue and orange, create strong separation.

Use: Make a subject stand out from its background.

Tip: Let one colour dominate and use the other as an accent.

Selected

Analogous

Neighbouring colours, such as blue, blue-green, and green, create a gentle relationship.

Use: Photograph a calm landscape or cohesive interior.

Tip: Look for a small contrasting detail to create a focal point.

Selected

Monochromatic

Different tints, tones, and shades of one hue give a photograph a focused visual identity.

Use: Simplify a scene with one dominant family of colour.

Tip: Use brightness contrast so the image does not become flat.

Selected

Triadic

Three evenly spaced colours form an energetic but balanced combination.

Use: Find lively street scenes, markets, or event details.

Tip: Reduce saturation in two colours to avoid visual overload.

White balance

Make whites look believable

White balance tells a camera how to interpret the colour of the light. It can make a scene look neutral, intentionally warm, or intentionally cool.

White BalanceTypical SituationCommon Visual Effect
Auto white balanceMixed or changing lightCamera estimates neutral colour; results can vary between scenes
DaylightClear daylightNatural outdoor colour without extra warming
CloudyOvercast scenes or shadeAdds warmth to counter cool blue light
TungstenTraditional warm indoor bulbsAdds blue to reduce an orange cast
FluorescentFluorescent indoor lightingCorrects common green or cool casts
