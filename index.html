What photography is really about

Photography is the practice of using light to create an image. A camera or smartphone collects light through a lens, records it with a sensor, and turns that information into a photograph.

Light affects brightness, colour, texture, and mood. Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO help you control the result. Smartphones automate many decisions, but they use the same underlying principles as DSLR and mirrorless cameras.

A useful mindset

Start by noticing light: where it comes from, how soft it is, and what it makes your subject look like. The settings follow the scene.

Core camera control

The Exposure Triangle

Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO work together to determine brightness. Changing one setting often means adjusting another.

Balanced Exposure

Aperture

Controls how much light enters the camera and affects depth of field.

Lower f-numbers mean a wider aperture and often a blurrier background. Higher f-numbers mean a narrower aperture and generally keep more of the image in focus.

Shutter Speed

Controls how long the sensor is exposed to light.

Fast speeds freeze motion; slow speeds create motion blur. Very slow exposures may need a tripod to keep the camera steady.

ISO

Controls the sensor's sensitivity or amplification of light.

Lower ISO usually gives cleaner images. Higher ISO brightens an image but can introduce visible noise.

Your tools

Camera and lens basics

Smartphone cameras

Portable cameras that combine multiple lenses and computational processing. A great way to practise light and composition every day.

DSLR cameras

Use a mirror and optical viewfinder. They offer interchangeable lenses and direct manual controls.

Mirrorless cameras

Use an electronic viewfinder or screen instead of a mirror, often in a smaller body with interchangeable lenses.

Wide-angle lenses

Include more of a scene. Useful for landscapes, architecture, and photographs that emphasise foreground space.

Standard lenses

Give a natural-looking field of view, making them flexible for everyday scenes and portraits.

Telephoto lenses

Bring distant subjects closer and narrow the view. Useful for sport, wildlife, and flattering portraits.
